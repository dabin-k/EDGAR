#### Headline: the latent-noise decomposition absorbs a 10× noise increase almost entirely
Split the noise axis in two, because **nl=0.0 uses a different code path** (`noisy=False`: no
latent-noise field, no `l_n` regularisation):

* **nl=0.0 → 0.01** costs 5–10× (discover-mean MSE 7.3e-8 → 7.6e-7 at h=4). This step

* **nl=0.01 → 0.1** is a clean 10× noise increase on one code path, and costs **nothing
  measurable**. Per-cell ratios across all 12 (h, D) pairs: 0.70, 1.74, 0.92, 0.63, 0.98, 1.29,

is robust to the seed scatter below.

#### Caveats — read before quoting any single cell
* **One seed per cell; scatter is ~3–5×** (decided: single seed + caveat, not extra runs).
  Two independent signs: the non-monotone noise ordering at h=1, and the h=1→h=2 MSE at
  D=0.005 / D=0.040 *falling* (0.6× / 0.2×) while the middle two samples rose ~3× as expected —
  the two that fell were exactly the two worst cells at h=1, i.e. regression to the mean.
  **Do not read individual-cell differences below ~5× as real.** Trends over many cells are fine.
* **skill rises with h** (h=4 ≥ h=2 ≥ h=1 nearly everywhere). This is *not* "longer rollouts are
  better" — the persistence floor degrades faster with horizon than the model's error does.
* **validate looks better than discover** (mse val ~10× lower) purely because validate is
  D {0.02, 0.04}, which decay faster and are easier. It is not a generalisation result — see
  asymmetry 2.

### Two asymmetries that block a naive 3-way comparison
1. **Scoring here is noise-independent by construction.** `teacher_forced_forecast` seeds from
   `u_clean` and scores against `u_clean`, so the persistence floor is *bit-identical* (2.566e-5)
   at nl=0.0 and nl=0.01 — noise affects only training. EDGAR scores against **noisy** targets
   (`load_data` feeds `u_noisy`), so its floor *rises* with noise level mechanically. Comparing
   the methods **across noise levels** is therefore invalid until this is reconciled. This is the
   concrete form of the General TODO above.
2. **"Validate" is not a generalisation test for STENCIL-NET.** One net per sample means it
   retrains on D {0.02, 0.04}; those are a second pair of *in-sample* scores. For EDGAR, validate
   is genuinely unseen D. `sweep.py` reports both means for alignment with EDGAR's reporting, but
   they are not like-for-like generalisation numbers and must not be plotted as if they were.

**Threshold 0.01 → 0.002.** The true `u_xx` coefficient *is* the sample's D, as low as 0.005, so

#### Weak SINDy was not reproducible — fixed
`WeakPDELibrary` draws its K=500 integration subdomains from the **global `np.random`** and
exposes no seed, so repeat fits on identical data returned different coefficients. Found by
re-running the sweep and diffing: all 12 weak fits differed, all 12 strong fits were
bit-identical. `_fit_weak` now seeds around library construction (restoring the caller's RNG
state after, so it stays a local effect); `fit_pdefind(..., seed=0)`. Two sweeps now agree
bit-for-bit.

Measured over 3 independent draws, the scatter this pins is **real, not numerical**:

| quantity | scatter across draws |
|---|---|
| `u_xx` at D=0.040 | ~1–2% (0.0386–0.0402) — trustworthy |
| `u_xx` at D=0.005, nl=0.1 | 0.0076 → 0.0131 (**72%**) |
| `uu_x` at D=0.020, nl=0.0 | −0.61 → −0.83 (**36%**) |
| `uu_x` at D=0.010, nl=0.1 | −0.18 → −0.34 (**84%**) |
| `skill_test` (any cell) | ≲2% — stable, so the crossover below is unaffected |

**Do not quote a weak coefficient to more than ~2 s.f. at high D, and treat low-D coefficients as
order-of-magnitude only.** Skill is stable because it reflects overall fit quality rather than any
one coefficient.

**The Nx=64 grid imposes a diffusion floor of ~0.012.** Weak SINDy returns `u_xx ≈ 0.012–0.014`
for *both* D=0.005 and D=0.010 — it is reporting the grid's own numerical diffusion, not the
physics — while recovering D=0.020 → 0.021 and D=0.040 → 0.039 well. So **any physical D below
~0.012 is unrecoverable on this grid**, at any threshold, at zero noise, and on every draw. The
advection coefficient collapses in lockstep (−0.38 at D=0.005 vs −1.04 at D=0.040) because the
under-resolved shock cannot be represented. This is closure error, not noise and not a tuning
artifact — and it is the one low-D conclusion that survives the subdomain scatter, since all
three draws put the floor in 0.008–0.014 while true D is 0.005.

Weak ≫ strong everywhere. Strong never recovers `u_xx` at D≤0.010 and returns `uu_x = −3.15`
(true −1.0) at D=0.005 — which is also the only instability: **2 unstable cells**, both
strong / D=0.005 / h=4, MSE capped at 1e3.

Noise: weak is essentially unaffected to nl=0.01 (the nl=0.0 and nl=0.01 rows agree to 3 s.f.),
then at nl=0.1 the high-D cell collapses toward the grid floor (0.0386 → 0.0166). Strong degrades
monotonically with noise at high D.

#### vs STENCIL-NET — a clean crossover (skill_test, h=1)
| method | nl | D=0.005 | D=0.010 | D=0.020 | D=0.040 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SINDy weak | 0.0 | 0.6870 | 0.9059 | 0.9876 | **0.9988** |
| STENCIL-NET | 0.0 | **0.9935** | **0.9996** | **0.9991** | 0.9956 |
| SINDy weak | 0.1 | 0.6918 | 0.9096 | 0.9894 | 0.9932 |
| STENCIL-NET | 0.1 | **0.9919** | **0.9977** | **0.9990** | **0.9970** |

**Where the physics is resolvable (D=0.040), SINDy weak beats STENCIL-NET** at low noise
(0.9988 vs 0.9956 at nl=0.0, 0.9988 vs 0.9952 at nl=0.01) — the correct symbolic form with 2 real
terms outperforms an 8897-parameter net. That edge is gone by nl=0.1 (0.9932 vs 0.9970), where
STENCIL-NET's latent-noise denoising wins. **Where the physics is not resolvable (D=0.005),
STENCIL-NET wins by a mile** (0.9935 vs 0.6870),
because it learns the effective coarse-grained operator and never needs a closed form.
That crossover is the benchmark's central result, and it is exactly the gap EDGAR is meant to
occupy. Note SINDy's skill *rises* with horizon (weak, D=0.005: 0.70 → 0.74 → 0.81 for h=1,2,4)
— again the persistence floor degrading faster than the model, not the model improving.
